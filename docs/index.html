Spillway ramp‑down planning and Nettleton stage model

This site hosts a ramp‑down decision‑support prototype for Glover Wilkins Lock and Dam and the supporting Nettleton gravel bar stage‑prediction methodology.

The tool is designed to compare immediate spillway closure with progressive gate reductions, highlight when navigation‑pool or inflow constraints limit gradual recession options, and provide transparent calculations for adaptive‑management trials in the Old Tombigbee River.

Methods and recommendations are summarized in the draft report Assessment of Spillway Operations, Flow Recession, and Mussel Habitat in the Old Tombigbee River.

  • Intended use. The ramp‑down decision‑support tool is a planning prototype for evaluating staged reductions in Wilkins spillway discharge after sustained releases to the Old Tombigbee River. It is meant to help operators and water managers compare an immediate spillway closure with alternative progressive gate‑closure sequences and to identify when navigation‑pool storage, inflow, or other constraints prevent a preferred gradual recession.

    Typical workflow.

    1. Confirm current conditions. Verify Wilkins spillway gate configuration (step, individual gates, or telemetry), Wilkins Pool elevation, Old River tailwater, and current or forecasted Bigbee discharge.
    2. Enter operating limits. Specify minimum allowable navigation‑pool elevation, target maximum downstream stage‑decline rate, number and timing of intermediate gate steps, and any known downstream resource constraints.
    3. Run comparison. The tool estimates spillway discharge from the gate‑step schedule and tailwater, generates a sequence of lower steps to Step 0, and compares a sudden closure versus a staged ramp‑down in terms of discharge, pool‑volume use, and predicted Nettleton depth.
    4. Review outputs. Proposed action times, target steps, gate settings, estimated spillway discharge, pool‑storage use, and predicted downstream depth are summarized for operator review before any operational trial.

    Caution. The current implementation is not an automated control system. Gate‑discharge estimates, storage calculations, downstream stage predictions, travel‑time assumptions, and ecological screening criteria remain subject to calibration and validation and should be interpreted accordingly.

  • The tool combines a Wilkins elevation–storage relation, a spillway gate‑step discharge table, and a linear Nettleton stage model to estimate how different ramp‑down schedules affect downstream water levels and gravel‑bar exposure.

    For a proposed sequence of gate steps, the model accounts for pool storage consumed above a minimum navigation‑pool elevation. When sufficient storage is available, it identifies a staged reduction intended to meet a user‑selected stage‑decline threshold; when storage is limiting, it flags the operation as navigation‑constrained and computes the fastest feasible reduction within the entered limits.

    The prototype does not yet fully represent travel time, tributary inflows, local runoff, floodplain storage, changing tailwater, or site‑specific differences in the relation between Wilkins spillway discharge and stage at Nettleton, Standifer Creek Hunting Club, Bigbee, and other habitats. Each operational trial should therefore be paired with post‑event monitoring of stage response, wetted area, exposed gravel bars, and mussel observations, with results used to refine the tool and its ecological thresholds.

    The report recommends a multi‑season adaptive‑management pilot to test whether staged spillway reductions can reduce recession rates at key mussel habitats after selected high‑flow events, and emphasizes that ramp‑down rates, hold durations, intermediate steps, and risk thresholds should remain provisional until evaluated against observed responses.

  • Operator‑verified inputs. The tool is designed to work with the following conditions entered or confirmed by operators:

    • Current Wilkins spillway gate configuration (step, gate openings, or telemetry).
    • Current Wilkins Pool elevation and Old River tailwater elevation.
    • Estimated or measured spillway discharge and forecasted/observed inflow.
    • Minimum allowable navigation‑pool elevation and target maximum downstream stage‑decline rate.
    • Number and timing of intermediate gate steps and any known downstream constraints or resource concerns.
    • Current or forecasted Bigbee discharge.

    Hydrologic data sources. Wilkins elevation–storage relations and spillway step tables are used to estimate pool‑volume use and discharge during ramp‑down. Upstream and downstream flows and stages are obtained from USGS Water Data for the Nation where available; users should cite USGS data products appropriately.[web:474][web:476]

    Contributing updates. Improved capacity curves, updated spillway rating information, and new ecological or monitoring data can be incorporated through pull requests to this repository, with brief documentation of methods and assumptions.
